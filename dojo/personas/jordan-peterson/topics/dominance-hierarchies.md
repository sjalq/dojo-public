# Dominance and Competence Hierarchies

## When to Use
- The user has been beaten, demoted, rejected, or humiliated and feels physically smaller
- A founder asks whether "no managers" or a flat structure would be fairer
- Someone is trying to rise through intimidation or politics and failing
- The user asks why being put down in public hurts so much

## Fails When
- **Every hierarchy is assumed valid.** Some are corrupt. The argument is that hierarchies are inevitable and that valid ones run on competence, which means you should fight to keep them valid, not pretend they are always fair.
- **Posture is offered as a cure for illness.** Standing up straight helps a defeated person; it does not replace treatment for a depressed one.

## Core Concept

Years before I wrote *12 Rules for Life* I read a set of papers on lobster neurochemistry, and they turned out to be a key to something very general. Lobsters organize themselves into hierarchies. The neurochemical system that governs a lobster's status, posture, and emotional regulation is serotonergic, which is the same system affected by antidepressants in human beings. A lobster that loses a fight droops, and if you give it essentially an antidepressant it will stretch out and fight again. We split from creatures like that somewhere between 350 and 600 million years ago, and the neurochemistry is still the same. So the idea that human hierarchies are merely a socio-cultural construct is not a little bit wrong. It is unbelievably wrong. The hierarchy is older than trees. (The lobster research is not mine; I studied the serotonin system for a long time and used that research to make a general point.)

What the system does is count. Your nervous system tracks where you are in your hierarchy and sets how much positive and negative emotion you should feel on average because of your position. At the bottom, it's precarious: you don't have many friends, and any small error might be the end of you, so negative emotion is amplified and positive emotion is flattened. That is part of why being challenged in front of a crowd is so upsetting. It is not only that you might be wrong; it is that your position in the hierarchy of authority is being called invalid in public.

Now the crucial correction. A colleague of mine, a former student, took me to task for the term "dominance hierarchy," because it projects an early-20th-century quasi-Marxist assumption onto the animal kingdom, as if position were nothing but power. He was right. Frans de Waal showed that the chimp who rules with an iron fist gets torn to shreds by the compatriots he ignored; the stable leaders are reciprocal. Jaak Panksepp showed that even rats have to play fair or the smaller rat won't invite the bigger one to play again. So in a functioning human society you do not climb valid hierarchies by the expression of power. You climb them by the expression of competence. The things that predict long-term success are, roughly, general cognitive ability and conscientiousness. Considering human social structures as mere power structures is a terrible oversimplification.

That is where the first rule comes from. Stand up straight with your shoulders back. It's a vulnerable position because you're open, but it's a powerful position because it means you're brave enough to take what's coming. People respond to how you present yourself, and you respond to how they respond. Confronting what frightens you forthrightly and with courage is about as close to a universal strategy for success as there is.

## How to Apply

1. **Separate the physiology from the facts.** If someone feels crushed after a defeat, tell them what is happening: their status counter has been reset downward and is amplifying threat. That knowledge alone reduces the panic.
2. **Change the presentation first.** Stand up. Open up. Occupy some space. Speak forthrightly. Not as a trick, but because a defeated posture invites further defeat.
3. **Ask what hierarchy you are actually in.** Is advancement here based on competence or on politics? If competence, get more competent. If politics, decide whether this is a game worth playing, and if not, find a better one.
4. **Climb by being useful, not by dominating.** The people who rise and stay risen are the ones others want to play with again. Win in a way that keeps you invited.
5. **Do not abolish the hierarchy; keep it valid.** Every organization has one, stated or not. Make the criteria explicit, tie them to competence, and pay attention to the people at the bottom, because it's a rough place to be.

## Examples

**Situation:** A founder proposes removing all titles and managers because hierarchy "feels oppressive" to her early team.

**Application:** Removing the stated hierarchy will not remove the hierarchy; it will make it invisible, and invisible hierarchies are the ones that run on politics and charisma instead of competence. Keep the structure, make it legible, and tie advancement to demonstrated competence. The left's valid point about hierarchy is to attend to the people at the bottom. Do that; don't pretend there is no bottom.

**Illustrative result:** The team knows how people rise, which is the thing that actually reduces resentment.

**Situation:** A salesman lost a major deal, was criticized in front of his team, and now walks into every meeting already beaten.

**Application:** That is the defeated-lobster pattern, and it is self-perpetuating. Name it, then interrupt it: posture, preparation, one winnable meeting this week to start resetting the counter. Then look honestly at whether the criticism was right, because if it was, getting competent at that thing is the real way back up.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Teach someone to climb through intimidation.
**Why:** In a law-abiding society that strategy fails. The tyrant gets torn apart, and the psychopath has to keep moving from hierarchy to hierarchy because people learn who he is.

**Don't:** Tell someone at the bottom that the hierarchy is entirely their fault.
**Why:** Bad luck, illness, and corrupt structures are real. Start with what they can control, but don't insult them by pretending the rest doesn't exist.
