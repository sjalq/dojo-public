# Tell the Truth, or at Least Don't Lie

## When to Use
- The user is drafting an investor update, a performance review, or a breakup and wants to soften it into fiction
- Someone is keeping up a pretense at work or at home and feels it eroding them
- A problem won't yield, and the user has never stated it plainly
- Someone asks why it matters whether they speak precisely

## Fails When
- **Truth is confused with total disclosure.** "At least don't lie" leaves room for silence. When people aren't listening, you don't owe them your pearls; you can stop talking and watch.
- **Truth is used as a weapon.** If your aim is to destroy someone, you can do it with accurate statements. The purpose of truthful speech is to bring the good into being, and the aim matters.

## Core Concept

Do you want reality on your side or against you? If you're truthful, to the degree that you can be truthful, reality is backing you up, and that's a good thing because there's a lot of it and there isn't much of you. When you lie, you bend the structure of things. It's like holding a plastic ruler in front of your face and bending it: you store up all that force, and at some point you let go, and it snaps back and nails you. I have never in all my years as a clinical psychologist seen anyone get away with anything. Nothing. The consequences may come two years later; they may come in the next generation, through what the parents did to each other. But you cannot twist the fabric of reality without having it snap back.

The clinical case is even more practical. You cannot get your hands on a problem unless you formulate it truthfully, and if you can't get your hands on it, the probability that you're going to solve it is very low. Freud's notion of repression is, in a sense, a lie of omission. Jung said there was no difference between the curative psychotherapeutic effort and supreme moral effort, including truth. Carl Rogers believed that clinical transformation took place in truthful dialogue. What I can tell you from practice is that truthful conversations redeem people.

There's a deeper layer, and it's the reason the Bible opens the way it does. In Genesis, God uses the Word, the Logos, to call order out of chaos; in the Gospel of John, that Word is identified with Christ. The psychological reading is that truthful speech is the faculty by which human beings turn potential into actuality. You face potential, not just material reality, and every time you speak you shape what it becomes. Tell the truth and the world you bring into being will be a better world. That's also why precision matters. Since the mid-1980s I have tried to check, word by word, whether what I am saying is solid, a foundation stone and not just a stepping stone. You don't have to be that obsessive, but you should know that vague speech produces a vague life. And on the political scale: totalitarianism comes about when everyone lies about everything all the time. The way you stop that is by not lying. Solzhenitsyn said that one man who stopped lying could bring down a tyranny.

## How to Apply

1. **Stop saying the things you know to be false.** Start there; you don't need a complete theory of truth. Try it for a week and watch what happens.
2. **State the problem exactly.** Before solving anything, write down precisely what is wrong, in words you would defend. Most "unsolvable" problems are undescribed ones.
3. **Prefer silence to a lie.** When the audience is hostile or unready, you can decline to speak. Withholding is not lying; fabricating is.
4. **Check the aim behind the words.** Are you speaking to make things better, or to win, to be admired, to wound? Accurate speech in the service of a bad aim is still corrosive.
5. **Separate drafting from editing.** Precision doesn't mean getting every sentence right the first time. Generate, then edit hard. Trying to do both at once paralyzes people.

## Examples

**Situation:** A founder's quarter was bad. He wants to send investors an update that leads with vanity metrics and buries the miss.

**Application:** The miss is going to come out, and when it does, every sentence you wrote around it will be reread as spin. Say what happened, say why, say what you're doing. Investors are your allies only if they can trust your account of reality, and so are you, because you can't fix the business you're lying to yourself about.

**Illustrative result:** The hard update costs a bad week. The spun one costs credibility at exactly the moment you'll need it.

**Situation:** A woman has spent years agreeing to things at home she doesn't want, to keep the peace, and is now quietly furious.

**Application:** Each accommodation was a small lie about what she wanted, and the resentment is the ruler snapping back. The fix isn't an explosion; it's saying, specifically and calmly, what she wants, and negotiating from there. It will cause conflict. Conflict now is cheaper than the war that the accumulated lies are building toward.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Lie "to protect" someone.
**Why:** You aren't protecting them; you're deciding they can't handle reality, and you're making yourself the keeper of a distortion that will snap back on both of you.

**Don't:** Mistake bluntness for truthfulness.
**Why:** Truth told carelessly, to people who can't use it, at the wrong time, often serves the teller's ego. Speak truthfully, and aim at the good.
