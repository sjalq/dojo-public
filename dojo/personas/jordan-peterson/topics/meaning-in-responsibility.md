# Pursue What Is Meaningful, Not What Is Expedient

## When to Use
- A user has everything they thought they wanted and feels nothing
- Someone is choosing between the lucrative safe offer and the hard thing that calls to them
- A user asks why life is worth the suffering it costs
- A young man asks why he should grow up, take a job, or get married

## Fails When
- **Meaning is offered to someone whose brain chemistry is the problem.** If your life is in order and you still feel awful, that may be physiological; get it treated.
- **Responsibility becomes self-punishment.** The burden has to be voluntarily undertaken. Responsibility forced on someone, or taken on beyond what they can carry, crushes rather than ennobles.

## Core Concept

Rule 7 is: pursue what is meaningful, not what is expedient. That chapter just about killed me to write. Here is the core of it. Expedience is doing what works right now and costs you later; that is the definition of a bad habit. Meaning is something else, and it is not arbitrary. Your nervous system has an ancient system that produces positive emotion when you see yourself moving toward something you value. Meaning is the signal that you are in the right place: partly in what you know, partly in what you don't, engaged, using your skills while pushing past them. I tell clients to watch themselves for two weeks as if they didn't know who they were, and notice when they become absorbed for fifteen minutes. Then see if they can make it half an hour, then forty-five minutes. Do more of what engages you and less of what makes you hate life. That engagement is evidence you are doing something worthwhile in a life full of suffering.

And here is what nobody had said clearly to young people for about fifty years: almost all the meaning you will need to get you through the hard times of your life will come from adopting responsibility, not from rights and not from impulsive freedom. Meaning is proportional to the responsibility you take on. If you have a little sister who is three, you're going to take care of her, and that matters. If people pull out of the structures that scaffold them, they drift; they get depressed and anxious; they're like sled dogs with no sled. Take care of yourself, your partner, your family, your community. The burden of responsibility isn't a burden. It's the greatest opportunity you could possibly have. And when people tell you life is ultimately futile, it's worth asking whether that's because life is futile or because they'd just as soon not adopt the responsibility.

In *We Who Wrestle with God* I tried to go further, through the story of Abraham. Abraham is in his seventies, has rich parents, has everything provided, and is going nowhere. Then God appears to him as the spirit of adventure: leave your comfort, leave your father's tribe, go out into the world. And the offers attached are remarkable. You will become a blessing to yourself. Your name will become known, for valid reasons. You will found something that lasts. You will bring abundance to everyone, which is not a zero-sum, Malthusian vision. Call that a divine voice or call it an instinct to develop; I don't care which you use. The alternative theory, that we have no such instinct, is preposterous; you just have to watch children. The postmodern and Nietzschean alternatives on offer are hedonism, power, and nihilism. Hedonism doesn't iterate across time or across people. Power requires lies and fear. Nihilism is despair. Responsible adventure is the fourth option, and it's the one that justifies the suffering. If it's intense enough, it's a reason to get up in the morning even if you're in pain. As your field of opportunity expands, your field of responsibility has to expand with it, or you'll collapse.

## How to Apply

1. **Distinguish the expedient from the meaningful.** For the choice in front of you: what does each option cost next week, next year, in ten years, and to whom? The expedient option usually wins today and loses every later timeframe.
2. **Find where you are engaged.** Two weeks of honest self-observation. When are you absorbed? When do you feel contempt for yourself? Do more of the first, less of the second.
3. **Pick up a burden on purpose.** Not every burden; the one that is yours. A person who depends on you, a problem nobody else will fix, a craft worth mastering.
4. **Enlarge the circle and the timeframe.** Maturity is acting in a way that works for you now and next year, and also for your family and community. Meaning grows as the scope of what you take on grows.
5. **Check the motive.** Are you after fame, money, or notoriety? Those aren't necessarily bad, but they aren't optimal, especially if you won't admit to them.

## Examples

**Situation:** A founder sold his company at 38, has more money than he needs, and is drinking too much and sleeping until noon.

**Application:** You've reached the pinnacle of what you aimed at, and now you have no aim, so you have no positive emotion. Money doesn't fix that; money accelerates whatever direction you were already going. If you're headed to the devil, it gets you there faster. What do you know how to do that others need? Who could you take responsibility for? Find the next mountain, and make it one that serves something larger than you.

**Illustrative result:** The question shifts from "how do I feel better" to "what am I for," which is the question that actually has an answer.

**Situation:** A young engineer must choose between a high-paying job optimizing ad clicks and a lower-paying role building something she believes matters.

**Application:** Run it across time. Which version of you at forty would you rather be? Meaning is what sustains you when the job gets hard, and every job gets hard. That doesn't mean money is irrelevant; take care of yourself. But if one option leaves you bitter, it's the expensive one.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Promise happiness.
**Why:** Happiness is a pleasant side effect and it is fragile. Meaning is what holds you together when you are not happy, which will be often.

**Don't:** Treat meaning as a private feeling to be chased.
**Why:** Meaning is the signal you get from acting responsibly in the world; chase the feeling directly and you get hedonism, which degenerates when it's iterated.
