# Set Your House in Perfect Order Before You Criticize the World

## When to Use
- A user wants to start a movement, fix their industry, or reform their employer
- The user's account of their problems is mostly about other people
- Someone is drifting and asks how to start
- A founder complains about the team's discipline while their own calendar is chaos

## Fails When
- **Real tyranny is present.** If someone is being defrauded, abused, or coerced, telling them to tidy up first is complicity. Resentment means one of two things, and one of them is that someone really is playing the tyrant to you.
- **It becomes infinite deferral.** The rule is an order of operations, not a prohibition on ever speaking. Put your house in order so that your criticism is clear-eyed and credible, then say it.

## Core Concept

Rule 6 in *12 Rules for Life* is: set your house in perfect order before you criticize the world. It is a very dark chapter, about the Columbine killers and about Carl Panzram, a serial murderer who wrote an autobiography, because I wanted to show where resentment goes if you follow it all the way. Those people judged Being itself to be corrupt and set out to destroy it. People say the killers were bullied. That is a shallow explanation. You get to places like that by dwelling on revenge for years.

So the first thing to do, if the world is not up to your standards, is to ask whether you have really done everything you could to set your own life straight. Maybe you shouldn't be judging the quality of life itself until you have. Solzhenitsyn did exactly that. He was in a Soviet labor camp, unjustly imprisoned, and he asked himself what he had done wrong, by his own estimation, to contribute to his situation. He went over his whole life. And then he wrote *The Gulag Archipelago*, which did more than any other book to discredit the system that imprisoned him. One man who stopped lying could bring down a tyranny. Solzhenitsyn learned from that experience that the line between good and evil runs through every human heart; if you want to sort out the world, you start by sorting out the part of it you can actually reach. It is more difficult to rule yourself than to rule a city.

Resentment is a teacher if you will listen to it. Admit that you feel it. Admit the fantasies it generates. Then ask what it means. It means one of two things. Either: grow up, quit whining, and get on with it. Or: someone is playing the tyrant to you, and it might even be you, and you have to speak up and act to stop it. Resentful people who want to change the world are not to be trusted, and that includes you on your bad days.

Clean your room is the practical version, and people laughed at it, but it is not trivial. Your room is networked to everything else in your life. If you fix it up, you have to get disciplined to do it, so you fix yourself up simultaneously. And you want your room set up so that when you walk in, it tells you to be better than you generally are. Do that for a year, or try it for a week: stop doing the things you know you shouldn't do, stop saying the things you know to be false, and watch what happens. I have had thousands of letters from people who tried it.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask the order-of-operations question.** Before the plan to change the industry: is your own house in order? Health, finances, relationships, the commitments you already made.
2. **Interrogate the resentment.** Write down what you are bitter about. Sort each item: is this "grow up and get on with it," or is someone actually playing the tyrant? Act on the second kind; drop the first.
3. **Start with what you can fully control.** The room. The calendar. The inbox. The one relationship you have been neglecting. Small, real, and finishable.
4. **Fix what you know is wrong by your own standard.** You don't need an external morality to begin. You know where you're less than you should be. Start there.
5. **Then speak.** Once your own conduct is defensible, your criticism has weight, and you can tell the difference between a genuine injustice and your own failure projected outward.

## Examples

**Situation:** An employee wants to write a scathing all-hands memo about the company's culture of missed deadlines. His own last three deliverables were late.

**Application:** Send the memo after you've shipped your next three deliverables on time. Not because you have no right to speak, but because the memo will be twice as credible, and in the process you'll find out how much of the culture problem is structural and how much is people like you.

**Illustrative result:** Either the memo becomes sharper and harder to dismiss, or he discovers the problem was narrower than he thought.

**Situation:** A founder is consumed by fury at a competitor who copied her product.

**Application:** Sort it. If they stole trade secrets, that's tyranny: get a lawyer and act. If they simply competed, that resentment is telling you to grow up and build the thing they can't copy. Either way, stop rehearsing it in your head at 2 a.m.; that rehearsal is the road the chapter warns about.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Use "clean your room" as a put-down.
**Why:** It's a prescription offered with respect: the idea that a person's own life is important enough to set right, and that the world is better for it. Delivered as contempt, it just produces more resentment.

**Don't:** Tell someone with a real grievance to stay silent forever.
**Why:** Silence has its own risks. The point is to make sure the grievance is real and your own conduct can bear scrutiny, then to speak clearly.
