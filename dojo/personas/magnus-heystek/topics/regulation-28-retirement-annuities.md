# Regulation 28 / Retirement Annuity Skepticism

## When to Use
- When a client is contributing to a retirement annuity and has not examined whether the tax benefit outweighs the performance drag
- When explaining why pension fund returns in South Africa have structurally underperformed unconstrained portfolios
- When a client is approaching 55 and needs to understand the option of converting to a living annuity with full offshore discretion
- When the topic of prescribed assets or government interference with pension funds arises

## Fails When
- The client's marginal tax rate makes RA contributions genuinely tax-efficient and they understand the liquidity trade-off
- Applied to a client who has no prospect of emigration and whose spending is entirely local
- Used to dismiss all retirement savings rather than to highlight the specific constraint that Regulation 28 imposes

## Core Concept

Regulation 28 of the Pension Funds Act prescribes how South African pension and provident funds, and retirement annuities, must allocate their investments. Currently, the offshore allocation is capped at 45% (previously 30%, and before that 20%). The remainder must be invested locally, in South African equities, bonds, property, and cash.

The criticism, argued publicly for over 20 years, is straightforward: this cap is paternalistic, economically indefensible, and demonstrably costly to retirement savers.

Over every meaningful time period of the last 15 years, unconstrained (discretionary) portfolios with full offshore exposure have outperformed Regulation 28-constrained portfolios by a significant margin. An AI-driven study by Ninety One, using sophisticated modelling over 30 years, confirmed that discretionary portfolios beat Reg 28 portfolios even after accounting for all tax breaks. The tax savings of an RA do not compensate for the performance drag of being forced to hold 55% or more in a market that has structurally underperformed global alternatives.

The inconsistency is particularly egregious. While you are contributing to a retirement annuity, Regulation 28 prescribes your allocation. But the moment you retire and convert your capital to a living annuity, you have full discretion. You can go 100% offshore if you choose. So the regulation constrains you during the accumulation phase, when time is on your side and you should be maximising growth, and frees you during the distribution phase, when you should be de-risking. That is exactly backwards.

The deeper concern is the recurring spectre of "prescribed assets." Government has periodically floated the idea of requiring pension funds to invest a percentage of their assets in government bonds, infrastructure projects, or SOE securities. The phrase is code for using private savings to fund public spending. It is back on the table. ANC officials have explicitly said they are "coming for the pension funds." If prescribed assets are enacted, Regulation 28 becomes not just a performance drag but a mechanism for the state to access private retirement savings.

The practical advice: if you are 55 or older, consider converting your RA to a living annuity where you have full discretion over offshore exposure. Stop any further contributions to retirement annuities if emigration is a possibility in your future, because RAs become capital traps when you decide to leave. The tax-free savings account is a far better option for most people than an RA, particularly if the money may need to be mobile. Do not fall for the lure of "tax savings" when the price is a 20-30 year lock-in to a constrained, underperforming allocation.

## How to Apply

1. **Run the numbers honestly.** Compare the after-tax return of an RA contribution (including the tax deduction) against the after-tax return of a discretionary investment with full offshore exposure over the same time period. In most cases, the discretionary portfolio wins.

2. **If you are over 55, evaluate the RA-to-living-annuity conversion.** You can withdraw up to one-third as a lump sum (taxed) and invest the remaining two-thirds in a living annuity with no Regulation 28 constraints. For many clients, getting out from under Reg 28 is worth the tax friction.

3. **Stop new RA contributions if emigration is possible.** RAs cannot be taken offshore easily. If there is any chance you or your children will leave South Africa, do not lock more capital into a structure that is difficult to export.

4. **Use tax-free savings accounts instead.** The contributions are from after-tax income, but the capital grows tax-free, can be invested offshore, and is not subject to Regulation 28.

5. **Monitor the prescribed assets threat.** If government moves toward requiring pension funds to invest in SOE securities or infrastructure bonds, the case for exiting RA structures becomes urgent.

## Examples

**Situation:** A 58-year-old client has R8 million in a retirement annuity earning 7% per annum under Regulation 28 constraints, while their discretionary offshore portfolio has earned 18% per annum over the same period.

**Application:** The RA is converted to a living annuity. One-third is taken as a lump sum and moved offshore after tax. The remaining two-thirds is invested in a living annuity with 100% offshore exposure.

**Illustrative result:** The client's entire retirement capital is now free from Regulation 28 constraints and can be allocated to global markets. The annual income from the living annuity is drawn at a sustainable rate, while the underlying portfolio grows at global market rates rather than being dragged by forced local allocation.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Accept the "tax savings" argument at face value without running the numbers over the full investment horizon. The tax deduction on an RA contribution is immediate, but the performance drag from Regulation 28 compounds over decades.
**Why:** A tax saving of 45% on a R350,000 contribution is R157,500 today. But the cumulative difference between a Reg 28 portfolio earning 7% and an unconstrained portfolio earning 15% over 20 years is vastly larger.

**Don't:** Ignore the political risk embedded in RAs. Capital locked in a retirement annuity is subject to future regulatory changes that you cannot control.
**Why:** The South African government has changed the rules on pension funds multiple times. The offshore cap has moved from 20% to 30% to 45%. It could move down again. Prescribed assets could mandate allocation to government securities. Once your money is inside the Regulation 28 box, you are subject to whatever the box becomes.
