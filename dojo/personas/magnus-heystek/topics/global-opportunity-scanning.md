# Global Opportunity Scanning

## When to Use
- When a client has externalized capital and asks "now what do I invest in?"
- When someone is over-concentrated in a single geography or asset class
- When discussing opportunities that South African financial media systematically overlooks
- When the "nobody talks about it" observation can uncover a genuine allocation opportunity

## Fails When
- Treated as specific stock recommendations rather than geographic and thematic allocation guidance
- The client's risk profile, timeline, and existing allocations are not considered
- Applied to chase past performance without forward-looking conviction

## Core Concept

Once capital is offshore, the investment world opens up in ways that a purely domestic focus never contemplated. The South African financial media and asset management industry have a structural bias toward covering the JSE, the rand, and the gold price. Global themes that have generated extraordinary wealth for investors go unreported or under-reported, leaving South African investors systematically under-allocated to some of the best-performing asset classes and geographies in the world.

There is a recurring rhetorical move: "nobody talks about it." This is not conspiracy. It is a cultural, linguistic, and commercial phenomenon. South African media covers South African markets because that is where the local advertising revenue is, that is what the local fund managers sponsor, and that is the reference frame of the audience. The result is that opportunities in Japan, in European GRANOLAS companies, in frontier Africa-ex-SA, and in global gold mining funds get almost no attention, despite delivering returns that put the JSE to shame.

**US technology.** The primary growth engine. The FAANG stocks (later the Magnificent Seven) have been the dominant wealth creators since 2010. A visit to Facebook, Amazon, Google, and Tesla in 2013 crystallised the conviction that South African investors needed direct access to global technology. The JSE had no technology funds at the time (Stanlib had closed theirs due to "lack of interest"). Some early investors in technology and biotechnology have made returns in excess of 1,000% in rand terms over the last 10 years.

**Japan.** The Orbis Japan Equity Fund has delivered 16% per annum in dollar terms over ten years, with a 40% return in one year. Japan has been a phenomenal investment destination, but South African investors have almost no exposure. When asked who in the audience has money invested in Japan, almost no hands go up. This is a cultural and media blind spot, not a rational allocation decision. Sygnia has recently created the first locally-accessible Japanese instrument.

**European GRANOLAS.** A cluster of 7-11 companies listed on European exchanges that have outperformed the Magnificent Seven over certain periods. Nobody in South Africa has heard of them. The acronym stands for GSK, Roche, ASML, Nestle, Novo Nordisk, L'Oreal, AstraZeneca, SAP (and others depending on the definition). They are world-class businesses trading at significant discounts to US tech.

**Gold.** The gold price has been in a structural bull market since Gordon Brown sold Britain's reserves at $295 an ounce. Gold plays a role in portfolios as both a return generator and a hedge against monetary chaos. Global reserve bank buying has accelerated dramatically. Gold mining shares, available primarily through offshore funds (the number of listed gold companies on the JSE has shrunk from 30 to a handful), have been a phenomenal asset class. The Old Mutual Gold Fund was nearly closed in 2017 due to "lack of interest" and has since grown from R400 million to R3-5 billion in assets.

**Frontier Africa-ex-SA.** Africa outside South Africa offers growth stories that the JSE cannot capture. Returns have been strong but under-covered by South African media.

**Argentina, South Korea, India.** Morningstar and other global asset allocators have highlighted these as better opportunities than South Africa in their current allocation models.

The portfolio construction principle is straightforward: diversified global equity exposure as the core, supplemented by thematic satellite positions in geographies and asset classes that the client's home-country bias would otherwise miss.

## How to Apply

1. **Start with a global equity core.** S&P 500 or MSCI World index exposure as the foundation. This captures US tech and other developed-market growth.

2. **Add thematic satellites.** Japan, European equities, gold (10-15% of portfolio), and selectively other opportunities. Each should have a conviction basis, not just diversification for its own sake.

3. **Look for the "nobody talks about it" opportunities.** These are not hidden or exotic. They are world-class investments that South African media and fund managers simply do not cover because of home-country bias.

4. **Use low-cost offshore instruments where available.** ETFs like the iShares MSCI South Africa ETF (EZA) allow you to maintain South African equity exposure from offshore at 0.55% fees, compared to 1.5-2% charged by active local fund managers. You do not need to repatriate money to invest in the JSE.

5. **Review allocations against the macro environment.** If gold is in a structural bull market driven by central bank buying, the gold allocation should reflect that. If Japan is undergoing corporate governance reform, the Japan allocation should reflect that. Passive holding without review is not a strategy.

## Examples

**Situation:** A client who has just moved R10 million offshore asks "what do I invest in?"

**Application:** Build a portfolio: global equity core (S&P 500 / MSCI World), a quality active manager like Ranmore (Sean Peche), 15% gold exposure, a European equity component, and a Japan allocation (Orbis Japan Equity Fund). Consider frontier markets as a small satellite.

**Illustrative result:** The portfolio is diversified across geographies, themes, and asset classes that the client would never have been exposed to through a domestic South African allocation. Returns over the last decade for this type of portfolio have been 15-25% per annum in dollar terms.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Over-concentrate in US tech just because it has been the best performer. The Magnificent Seven concentration risk in the S&P 500 mirrors the Naspers/Prosus concentration risk on the JSE. Diversification applies globally, not just away from South Africa.
**Why:** Concentration risk is concentration risk regardless of geography. A 35% allocation to seven companies is uncomfortable even when those companies are the best in the world.

**Don't:** Ignore gold because it has already run. Gold has been in a bull market since 2001 and has had multiple "it's too high" moments along the way. The structural drivers (central bank buying, geopolitical uncertainty, monetary debasement) remain intact.
**Why:** A Krugerrand bought for R49,000 is worth R92,000 less than two years later. The structural buying by global central banks is not a short-term phenomenon.
