# Political/Policy Risk Screen

## When to Use
- When a South African investment decision must account for sovereign and policy risk
- When specific legislation (EWC, NHI, BELA Act, prescribed assets) creates direct portfolio risk
- When coalition politics (GNU stability, ANC-EFF-MK possibilities) could change the regulatory environment for investors
- When someone says "the politics will sort itself out" and you need to show why that is not an investment thesis

## Fails When
- Political commentary becomes an end in itself, detached from an actionable investment recommendation
- Applied as permanent doom without acknowledging that the tactical read should change when evidence changes (as it did briefly with the GNU in mid-2024)
- Used to make election predictions rather than to screen existing and probable risks

## Core Concept

Political and policy risk in South Africa is not background noise. It is a direct portfolio input. Treating it as something that "doesn't affect my investments" is the single most expensive mistake a South African investor can make.

The risk is tracked across several specific categories, each with direct investment implications:

**Expropriation Without Compensation (EWC).** The EWC Bill was signed into law. It empowers approximately 400 ANC officials across all levels of government to expropriate property (not only land) in the broadly defined "public interest." Verbal testimony in a Lands Court carries the same weight as documentary evidence. The ANC's track record with wide discretionary powers, as documented by the Zondo Commission, does not inspire confidence that these powers will be used judiciously. The direct investment implication: property assets outside the Western Cape carry confiscation risk that is no longer theoretical.

**National Health Insurance (NHI).** The NHI Act threatens to restructure the private healthcare system. The investment implication depends on implementation detail that remains unclear, but the risk is that medical aid contributions become redundant, private hospital groups lose their revenue base, and the quality of healthcare available to those who currently use private facilities deteriorates.

**Prescribed assets.** The recurring proposal to require pension funds to invest a percentage of assets in government bonds, infrastructure projects, or SOE securities. This would turn Regulation 28 from a performance drag into a mechanism for the state to access private savings. ANC officials have said explicitly that they are "coming for the pension funds."

**Exchange control risk.** The foreign investment allowances (R2 million single discretionary, R10 million with tax clearance) are allowances, not rights. They can be repealed overnight by a simple regulation published in the Government Gazette. An ANC-EFF-MK coalition or a collapse of the GNU could lead to tightening or elimination of these allowances. Once they are gone, the window for externalizing capital closes.

**BEE and mining policy.** BEE requirements increase the cost and complexity of doing business in South Africa. Mining policy under certain ministers has driven exploration expenditure to 0.6% of global spending. Global mining is a comparative business: companies compare South Africa with Australia, Canada, and Chile, and walk away.

The tactical read is evidence-responsive, not fixed. When the GNU formed in mid-2024, things appeared to be getting marginally better, and selective local exposure made sense. When the improvement proved narrow (commodity-driven, not fundamentals-driven), the tactical read reverted. The strategic assessment, that South Africa carries too much sovereign risk for concentrated investment, has not changed in 15 years because the structural conditions have not changed.

The phrase that captures the approach: "Plan for the worst and hope for the best." Not the other way around.

## How to Apply

1. **Track each risk category separately.** EWC, NHI, prescribed assets, exchange controls, mining policy, coalition stability. Each has different probability and different portfolio impact. Do not lump them into a single "political risk" bucket.

2. **Translate each risk into a portfolio action.** EWC risk means reducing property exposure outside the Western Cape. Prescribed assets risk means reducing RA contributions. Exchange control risk means using allowances now rather than later. Each risk has a specific hedge.

3. **Update the tactical read when evidence changes.** If the GNU delivers genuine reform, the tactical allocation can shift toward selective local exposure. If the GNU collapses, the allocation shifts further offshore. The strategic diversification thesis does not change, but the tactical weighting does.

4. **Do not wait for the risk to materialize before acting.** By the time EWC is being enforced, by the time exchange controls are tightened, the opportunity to act has passed. The time to move capital offshore is while the allowances exist and the rand has not yet collapsed.

5. **Treat political risk as investment fact, not political opinion.** The question is not whether the ANC is good or bad. The question is whether the policy environment creates risk for capital.

## Examples

**Situation:** The EWC Bill is signed into law. A client with four investment properties in Gauteng asks whether to sell.

**Application:** The political risk screen. EWC empowers local officials to expropriate property. The properties are in a jurisdiction with deteriorating governance, declining property values, and no effective rule of law enforcement. The long-term trend in Gauteng property values (outside a few premium nodes) has been flat to negative in real terms for a decade.

**Illustrative result:** Sell the properties, pay the taxes, externalize the capital, and invest in a diversified global portfolio. The proceeds are worth far more deployed offshore than sitting in a jurisdiction with confiscation risk and declining real values.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Dismiss political risk as "just politics" that will not affect your investments. South African political decisions have direct, measurable effects on currency, property values, fund performance, and capital mobility.
**Why:** The Nene firing in 2015 moved the rand 6% in a single day. State capture cost the economy trillions. EWC is law. These are not abstract political debates. They are investment facts.

**Don't:** Freeze into permanent worst-case positioning without updating the read. The thesis is evidence-based, not ideological.
**Why:** If the evidence genuinely changes, the position should change. A permanent-bear who ignores improvement is as wrong as a permanent-bull who ignores deterioration. Both are substituting ideology for analysis.
