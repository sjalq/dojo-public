# AMD Pattern (Accumulation / Manipulation / Distribution)

## Attribution
AMD / PO3 (Power of Three) is ICT / Smart Money Concepts lineage language for how smart money accumulates, traps, and distributes. Fefe operationalizes it as a chart pattern with stairs/elevator path shape, HTF order-block magnets, 15m rejection, and half-size then add trade management. Present it as his process model with that lineage credited, not as a claim of original invention of PO3.

## When to Use
- Price is building into a higher-timeframe order block (hourly+ supply/demand)
- You see elevator down then stairs up (or the inverse for longs) into that magnet
- Retail is drawing trendlines and parking stops under them
- You need one pattern language for trap → reverse that works in crypto and other liquid markets

## Fails When
- There is no defined HTF order block or strong level (three consecutive candles / clear rejection zone)
- You short every push into a zone without LTF rejection and a close against the trap
- You treat the first wick as mandatory full size and skip the retrace-to-initiation add
- News / regime shock makes pure TA less reliable; still size down and demand structure

## Core Concept

AMD is accumulation, manipulation, and distribution. Same idea as Power of Three (PO3): identify how smart money traps retail, buys at the right time, and sells at the right time. You can see all of it on the chart.

Textbook short-side shape (from the canonical teaching trade):

1. **Accumulation.** After an elevator down, price stairs back up in a choppy climb. Market makers want retail long on the lower timeframes while building a beautiful uptrending trendline so stops sit under that line. Accumulation often sits just below the HTF order block.
2. **Manipulation.** An impulsive push into (and sometimes through) the HTF order block. Breakout traders believe the hourly high is breaking. Price can wick higher without a true close (liquidity sweep). That is the trap.
3. **Distribution.** Rejection and a real close against the trap on the lower timeframe (e.g. 15m close back below). Price sends the opposite way of the retail breakout.

Path language: elevator down + stairs up is the usual accumulation path into the magnet. Follow market structure on the HTF first; AMD is how you enter the continuation or reverse at the magnet, not a reason to ignore structure.

Nobody knows what the market will do. You trade probabilities. A clean AMD into a real HTF level is a high-probability pattern language when the rejection confirms.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the HTF magnet.** Prefer a strong order block / supply zone: last opposing candle before an impulsive move, or a level with at least three consecutive candles that mark real strength. Mark it on 1h/4h/daily as appropriate.
2. **Drop to LTF for path.** On 15m (or 5m if crowded), look for stairs into the magnet and trendline liquidity under the climb.
3. **Wait for manipulation.** Impulsive push into/through the zone. Optional liquidity sweep: wick beyond, no hold/close.
4. **Demand distribution proof.** Clear rejection: not only a wick, but a close below (for shorts) on the LTF. That is when the AMD is "clear."
5. **Manage size in two phases.** On first clean rejection, take about half the intended size so you do not miss an impulsive open (e.g. New York) continuation. Wait for retrace into the initiation order block of that distribution leg; on second rejection, add and trail.
6. **Trail with structure.** Mark successive LTF order blocks, add or tighten only on reaction, until a real reversal signal (e.g. clear higher high / W formation against the short).

## Examples

**Situation:** Hourly supply formed by three consecutive candles and a last bullish candle before an impulsive dump. Price later stairs up under that box.

**Application:** Mark the 1h order block. On 15m, wait for choppy accumulation, impulsive push into the box, then close back below. Post "massive AMD," take half short on first rejection, add on retrace to the 15m initiation OB.

**Illustrative result:** A structured short that rides distribution legs without needing to predict the first tick of the dump.

**Situation:** Live daily update: "there's an AMD happening" into a higher-timeframe order block, then 15m rejection.

**Application:** Treat the call as process, not hopium. If 15m rejects, short scalp or swing per plan; if structure later prints a higher high against you, flatten and reassess.

**Illustrative result:** AMD becomes a repeating live language, not a one-off backtest story.

**Situation:** Guest recount of calling a top with AMD.

**Application:** Same phases: build, trap into level, distribute. Use as narrative confirmation that the model is how he describes real calls, still NFA and still confirmation-first.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Short or long the manipulation breakout because "it's breaking the hourly."

**Why:** That is the retail trap the pattern is built on. Facts are closes and structure; feelings are "it has to break out."

**Don't:** Enter full size on the first wick with no close.

**Why:** Wicks can be sweeps. Distribution wants a real LTF close against the trap, then optional add on the retrace.

**Don't:** Force AMD labels when there is no HTF order block.

**Why:** Without a magnet, chop is just chop. The pattern's edge is trap-at-level, not pattern-for-pattern's-sake.

**Don't:** Hold through a clear LTF structure reversal because the AMD "should" keep going.

**Why:** When structure flips (e.g. clear higher high / W against the short), the job is to close and run the process again, both directions.
