# Liquidity and Retail Traps

## Attribution
Liquidity-sweep and stop-run ideas sit in broader ICT / Smart Money Concepts education. Fefe operationalizes them with freehand stairs into magnets, retail trendline stops, and breakout-feeling traps inside AMD. Credit the lineage; use his stairs/elevator and close-vs-wick rules as the process layer.

## When to Use
- Stops cluster under trendlines or above obvious highs
- Stairs climb into an order block while retail longs
- Breakout traders chase a push that lacks a hold/close

## Fails When
- Every wick is called engineered liquidity with no structure context
- User refuses all breakouts even when MSB/4-point confirmation is real
- Trap language replaces risk management

## Core Concept

Retail amateur pattern: trade trendlines, park stops under the trendline, get "wigged" or stopped, then watch price reverse. Market makers know this. When price does elevator down and stairs up into an HTF order block, the stairs often form an uptrending trendline that harvests that behavior. That built-up liquidity is fuel.

Manipulation in AMD is the impulsive push into/through the level that makes breakout traders believe the hourly high is breaking. Price can run higher as a **liquidity sweep** without a real close, then distribute the other way. Trading on the feeling that "we're definitely breaking out" is the trap. Facts are closes, structure, and rejection.

Liquidity language is not mystical. It is: obvious stops, obvious breakout entries, and path shapes that walk people there.

## How to Apply

1. **Find the obvious retail line.** Trendline under stairs, equal highs, session high into supply.
2. **Ask who is trapped if we sweep it.** Longs under the line? Breakout buyers above the high?
3. **Prefer confirmation after the sweep.** Wick beyond + close back inside/against = sweep narrative. Close and hold beyond may be real MSB: do not auto-fade every break.
4. **Couple to HTF magnet.** Traps are highest quality into real order blocks, not mid-air.
5. **Do not place your own stops at the most obvious cluster** if your model allows a slightly more structural invalidation beyond the noise.
6. **Journal trap entries you almost took.** The almost-trade is the lesson.

## Examples

**Situation:** 15m stairs up with a clean rising trendline into hourly supply; stops clearly under the line.

**Application:** Expect hunt risk under the line into the OB. Wait for push into OB and distribution close; do not be the trendline long.

**Illustrative result:** You are positioned for the trap, not inside it.

**Situation:** Hourly wick above the high into supply, no hourly close, 15m closes back down.

**Application:** Liquidity sweep + distribution language. Short plan with stop beyond the sweep extreme per risk rules.

**Situation:** Close above previous high after 50% retrace with full 4-point sequence.

**Application:** That can be real MSB, not a trap to fade automatically. Trap model yields to confirmation model when closes stick.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Be the trendline long with stops in the crowded basement.

**Why:** That is the textbook liquidity the stairs are building.

**Don't:** Call every green breakout candle manipulation.

**Why:** Sometimes structure breaks for real. Require the full read.

**Don't:** Move stops tighter into the liquidity pool right before the magnet.

**Why:** You volunteer to be the fuel.
