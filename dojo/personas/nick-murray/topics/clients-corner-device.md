# The Client's Corner Device

## When to Use
- When an advisor needs to send a written piece directly to clients or prospects
- When an advisor wants to reinforce the long-term investing thesis proactively
- When writing a letter or essay to preempt client panic during or ahead of a market event
- When drafting client communications that translate the advisor's philosophy into client-accessible language

## Fails When
- The essay is sent in place of a personal conversation rather than alongside one
- The tone remains advisor-facing (insider language, industry jargon) rather than client-facing (warm, direct, personal)
- The advisor sends generic content without connecting it to the specific client's plan and goals

## Core Concept

The Client's Corner is a recurring essay format in Nick Murray Interactive, written by Murray specifically for advisors to forward or hand directly to clients, verbatim. It is not an advisor-facing piece repurposed for clients. It is written from the start in a different voice register: warmer, more personal, addressed to "you" the investor rather than "you" the advisor.

The device serves several purposes. First, it lets the advisor proactively reach out to clients with a polished, carefully crafted message rather than improvising under pressure. Second, it reinforces the advisor's philosophy by having it stated by someone other than the advisor, providing validation and a different angle on the same truth. Third, it demonstrates the advisor's ongoing engagement with their clients' wellbeing, even in months where no portfolio action is needed.

The essays cover core themes: the difference between the planner and the forecaster, the power of staying invested through crises, the distinction between the stock market and the great companies, and the emotional cost of selling at the bottom. They are typically 400-600 words, written in Murray's direct, declarative style but accessible to a reader who knows nothing about finance beyond their own experience.

The Client's Corner is a drafting-mode deliverable. An advisor can model their own client letters on this pattern: short, thesis-driven, emotionally resonant, ending on a note that reinforces the plan. The key is the voice shift: from teaching advisors to speaking to investors.

## How to Apply

1. **Use the Client's Corner as a template for your own client letters.** Open with a specific, timely observation. Connect it to the timeless thesis. Close with a reaffirmation of the plan. Keep it under 600 words. Write it as if speaking to a smart friend who does not work in finance.

2. **Send proactively, not reactively.** The best time to send a Client's Corner piece is before the crisis, or in the first days of a decline, not after the client has already panicked. Proactive communication is a trust-builder. Reactive communication is damage control.

3. **Personalize the delivery.** Do not blast-email the essay to your entire client list as a mass communication. Send it with a personal note: "I was reading this and thought of our conversation last month. The author says what I've been saying to you, but better than I could."

4. **Maintain the voice distinction.** Advisor-facing content uses insider language, industry references, and tactical advice. Client-facing content uses personal pronouns, everyday analogies, and emotional resonance. Never send advisor-facing content to a client.

## Examples

**Situation:** An advisor wants to preempt panic as tariff uncertainty rattles markets.

**Application:** Write (or adapt) a Client's Corner piece: "Your advisor is not an economic forecaster or a market prognosticator. They are a very long-term planner. The twists and turns of the economy and the gyrations of the market have nothing to do with whether your long-term plan succeeds or not. A top quality advisor does not predict; they plan. And regardless of what else is going on in the world, if your goals haven't changed, your advisor will tell you not to change your plan. Let your advisor be good at what they are good at, charting a long-term course that has always succeeded over time, rather than asking them to be good at what nobody is good at: foretelling the immediate future."

**Illustrative result:** The client receives a polished, reassuring piece that reinforces the advisor's message before fear takes hold.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Use the Client's Corner as your only communication channel.
**Why:** Written essays complement personal contact. They do not replace it. A client in crisis needs to hear the advisor's voice, not read an essay.

**Don't:** Write the essay in a detached, academic tone.
**Why:** The client should feel spoken to personally. "Your advisor" works. "The financial advisory community" does not. Write to the individual, not to the market.
